## 1. Executive summary

1. **The design is coherent but not yet implementable, and not yet shippable as immutable money.** Documentation and design are far ahead of code. The Rust is an executable specification with a solid token ledger (`kaspa_tokens.rs`) and an unfinished economic core. The covenant oracle is a measured prototype, not a working system.
2. **A new blocker was found in the oracle work.** As written, a transaction cannot co-spend a vault and an oracle UTXO: the vault demands zero oracle outputs, the oracle demands one. "Both variants compile and execute" covers compilation and a mass ladder, never a vault+oracle transaction. *[rev. Nov 10]* Reproduced by execution for both variants and both entries (`liquidate`, `withdraw`); a fix declaring the oracle outputs was executed and accepted by the script engine for both variants (§6, P1). [V-lead, executed]
3. **The headline simulation figures are partly not reproducible.** "kFIAT 2 %" at −85 % matches no statistic of the documented configuration; the committed result files do reproduce exactly. The stated reason for rejecting a 250 % ICR (it makes things worse) is not reproduced: 250 % is better in the repo's own model. Pegged-ICR and floor conclusions rest on two unmeasured parameters that move ENRK p95 loss by a factor of ~240.
4. **Your carry-over finding holds in direction.** Uncapped socialisation to borrowers brings the freeze back (latent p95 16 % vs 0 % at 5 %/day capacity); capped variants are within noise of the waterfall. The mechanism and its dependence on the capacity assumption are explained in §4.
5. **kFIAT is undefined on three axes:** denomination (par vs market), incentive (nothing forces or rewards holding it), and settlement (waterfall not implemented). Every published figure assumes par.
6. **Stale values survive in code.** `types.rs` and `lib.rs` still carry the 85 % floor and the 80/20 split; `PROJECT_MANIFEST.md` (gitignored) still carries Band/Chainlink, 85 %, 80/20 and optional governance.
7. **Verdict on feasibility (§8):** an L1 CDP is expressible with today's primitives on a testnet; the economic layer is viable as an immutable system only at small, capped scale and only under conditions listed in §8. A hard debt ceiling sized from measured KAS liquidity is the cheapest hedge available and is not in the design today.

## 4. Loss absorption: does your conclusion hold?

500 paths per cell, frozen configuration, `loss_absorption_analysis.py absorption --paths 500`, −85 %:

| Policy | ENRK p95 | kFIAT p95 | latent p95 |
|---|---|---|---|
| A waterfall (kFIAT, then ENRK) | 4.1 | 38.9 | 0.0 |
| B1 borrowers first, no cap | 0.0 | 13.9 | 16.0 |
| B2 same, equal allocation | 0.0 | 14.3 | 15.7 |
| B3 cap 5 % | 4.3 | 38.6 | 0.0 |
| B4 cap 15 % | 4.4 | 39.4 | 0.0 |
| C3 75 % to borrowers | 3.0 | 33.0 | 5.9 |
| D1 kFIAT, borrowers (cap 10 %), ENRK | 3.5 | 40.4 | 0.0 |
| D2 kFIAT, borrowers (no cap), ENRK | 2.5 | 42.4 | 0.0 |

**Conclusion: it holds in direction, with three caveats.**

1. *Mechanism.* Pushed debt lands on already-underwater vaults and consumes liquidation capacity again when they are re-liquidated; in the waterfall, written-off debt leaves the system and frees capacity. On total unbacked (0.3 × kFIAT + 0.7 × ENRK + latent), the means are A 3.5 vs B1 3.7: uncapped carry-over turns realised loss into latent loss, it does not remove it.
2. *Capacity dependence.* B1 latent p95 is 29.1 (every path frozen) at 2.5 %/day, 16.0 at 5 %, 0.0 at 10 %; the waterfall is 3.6 / 0.0 / 0.0. The "freeze returns" result is entirely a statement about the assumed capacity.
3. *"Capped changes almost nothing" is partly an artefact of the cap basis* (I17): the nominal 5 % binds at ~2 % of opening debt and relieves kFIAT by almost nothing (mean 10.0 → 9.8). At −95 %, a 15 % cap reopens a small hole (latent p95 4.3). A cap defined on a fixed basis should be re-run before concluding.

Other levers at −85 % (ENRK p95): kFIAT share 0/15/30/50 % → 12.0/7.7/4.1/0.6; opening ICR 160/180/200/250/300 % → 10.7/7.2/4.1/3.3/2.4; floor 85 % + redemption → ENRK 0.0 but latent p95 26.4.

Seed noise at 500 paths: ENRK p95 ±0.5, kFIAT p95 and mean ±1, kFIAT median up to ±1.3. Use ≥ 500 paths and never quote the median.

## 6. Oracle and covenants: findings and proposed solutions

*Proposals are technical options, not design decisions.*

**P1. Vault + oracle co-spend (B1).** *[rev. Nov 10: executed — see result below]* Proposal (compiled with the repo's prebuilt `argentc` at Argent `e76ee07`): in **both** `liquidate` and `withdraw`, declare `outputs { round: OracleFeed::PriceRound }` and require `oracle.outputs become { round <- PriceRound(state(oracle.inputs.round)) }` (the `minter.ag` / icc-semantics pattern). The generated script then requires `OpCovOutputCount == 1` plus `validateOutputStateWithInputTemplate`. Cost: vault script 805 → 964 B; `liquidate` sigscript 815–831 → 974–990 B. Alternative (compiled): one app with one covenant id where the vault is a leader that `consumes` and re-`emits` a price follower — vault 660 B, no covenant-id binding needed, but each liquidation transaction is closed to one vault plus one follower. [V-agent] Derived: a liquidation transaction is ~1.6 KB (~6.4 k transient), so by transient mass alone ~200 vaults could share an oracle input per transaction; vault compute mass is unmeasured. [D]
*Execution result [rev. Nov 10, V-lead]:* with the declared-outputs edit applied to both entries in both variants, a transaction spending one vault and one price UTXO (`use`) and recreating the price UTXO unchanged was **accepted by the script engine** for `liquidate` (ICR 100 %) and for a signed `withdraw`; a healthy vault (ICR 1000 %) is refused by the script on `liquidate`; a vault pointing at a different oracle covenant id is refused. Originals are refused as described under B1. *Further cases executed [rev. Nov 10, same day, V-lead; identical in variants A and B unless stated]:*
- **Withdrawal boundary:** withdrawing down to exactly 200 % ICR is accepted; to 199 %, to 150 % and a zero withdrawal are refused.
- **Price tampering:** co-spending the oracle through `use` while recreating it at a lower price (A: lower value; B: lower price field) to make a healthy vault liquidatable is refused. The refusal is reported on input 0 (the fixed vault, which pins the recreated oracle state); the oracle's own `use` guard was not isolated.
- **Publisher `sweep`** (1 → 2 split, round + 1): accepted when signed by the publisher (A: price 1.0 → 0.5 as two children of value 0.5; B: value conserved, price field changed); refused when signed by another key. **Sweep to price 0 is accepted in variant A and refused in variant B** (B requires `next_price > 0`; A's `unrestricted(left.value)` has no lower bound — see I26).
- **Mass and relay fee of one fixed liquidation transaction** (vault + price input + recreated price output): A compute 2,053 / transient 6,732 / storage 0; B compute 2,249 / transient 7,516 / storage 0. Against block limits 500,000 / 1,000,000 / 500,000 that is 0.4 % of compute and 0.7 % of transient mass per liquidation. Minimum relay fee about 0.0035 KAS (A) and 0.0039 KAS (B), using an approximate size (the size estimate in the test is rough; the compute mass is exact for the built transaction). Real signatures, a real fee output and auction distribution rules are not included, so these are lower bounds.
Not tested: any node (testnet-10), miner inclusion, N-input rounds with real signatures, an oracle round with many vaults sharing one price input, the Silverscript #252 pattern in isolation. The tests live only in the session scratchpad (§11, §13.4).

**P2. Q1c and the minting binding.** The reframing in `ORACLE-BINDING.md` (the binding to secure is asset → vault; a forged vault is inert unless it can mint) is logically sound. Vault → oracle is solvable by lineage because `spawns` makes consensus derive the child id from the parent outpoint. A design sketch (untested): all vaults are UTXOs of one covenant id V; new vaults continue from "nursery slot" UTXOs that copy `oracle_id` and `asset_id`; the `MinterProxy` then needs a single controller id. **Asset → vault minting is not solved by any shipped pattern** (Argent's bootstrap-with-spawns and launch proofs are follow-ups). New hazard reasoned from the Argent docs (untested): `co_spent()` proves only that some V input is present, and entries on one actor are indistinguishable, so an attacker might co-spend the proxy with any V UTXO in an unrelated entry and mint freely. Mitigations to test: every non-mint V entry forbids the asset id, or a small mint-gate covenant with M lanes. Burn/repay has no pattern at all (KCC-20 has no burn). The question for core developers should be narrowed to "presence vs scope" of co-spend proofs. [V-agent for the facts it read; the sketch is design]

**P3. Round atomicity.** Atomicity is not sufficient: a price UTXO has no expiry and Kaspa offers only lower bounds on time, so a stale UTXO the publisher skips stays valid forever and an owner could withdraw against a stale price. The sweep must provably consume every live follower. Compiled: an N-way `sweep_all` with a leader and followers; single-actor form grows ~215 B per slot (27.9 KB at N = 128) on every spend; two-actor form at N = 128 is a 99.9 KB leader used once per round and a 514 B follower. Derived: ~740 k of 1 M transient mass at N = 128, upper bound ~160 slots per atomic round, fee ~0.37 KAS per round (~3,200 KAS/day at one round per 10 s). The honest baseline to compare against is a single price UTXO (N = 1): no stale copies, but contention. Leader-ness of the oracle is harmless because a vault transaction touches one price UTXO. [D, compiled]

**P4. Mass limit.** *[rev. Nov 10: resolved in favour of "no 100 k cap"; the first version's N ≈ 120–130 is withdrawn.]* The 100,000 standard-mass constant (`MAXIMUM_STANDARD_TRANSACTION_MASS_PRE_TOCCATA`, `mining/src/mempool/check_transaction_standard.rs:26` in v2.0.x) was lifted 30 minutes before Toccata activation and deleted in commit `02dcf442` (2026-08-20), present in v2.1.0 (2026-09-22, latest tag) and master. Mempool policy now rejects a transaction only if it can never fit a block (compute > 500 k, transient > 1 M, storage > 500 k, or gas over the lane limit). [V-agent, source read] The transient ceilings 642 / 532 therefore stand as arithmetic. What remains unresolved is **inclusion**, not standardness: miner selection is probabilistic and weighted by (fee / max(compute, 0.5 × transient, storage))³, so a transaction close to a full block is disadvantaged and no inclusion guarantee exists (issue #46 Q5; the audit's earlier label "Q4" is the numbering of the posted text). Still to test on testnet-10 (needs a funded testnet key; see decision 15).

**P5. `icr_percent` (I18).** Guards for `debt == 0`, bounded inputs, pre-scaled cross-multiplication or a two-limb multiply, plus property tests at extremes. The overflow threshold is price-dependent, so any bound must be stated against the price scale, not as "9.2 KAS".

**Toccata.** Active on mainnet at DAA 474,165,565, about 2026-06-30 (rusty-kaspa `params.rs:724`, v2.0.0 notes); the documents' "June 5–20" dates are wrong and should be corrected (docs.kaspa.org/toccata; rusty-kaspa `docs/toccata-guide.md`). *[rev. ## 7. Economic options (proposals for owner decision)

Research quality: Liquity v1/v2 redemption and Stability Pool mechanics were read from primary documentation [V-agent]; Maker, Reflexer, crvUSD, Aave, Ethena, Inverse/DOLA and KAS market figures are secondary; Angle, Frax, Uniswap and Terra details were not researched [U]. Sources are listed in the working reports.

**A. kFIAT denomination.** A1 fixed face in peg units (deletes `kfiat_price`, keeps published numbers valid, no pricing signal). A2 fixed face plus an on-chain monotone write-down index driven by `kfiat_uncovered` (no second oracle; needs global state, an open question on UTXO). A3 marked to market (second oracle, ICR reflexivity, invalidates the stress results). *Proposal:* A2, with A1 as the minimal fallback. *To simulate:* kFIAT discount as a model output and a borrower mint-choice model.

**B. Loss absorption.** B1 waterfall by write-down (kFIAT, then an ENRK index): no cascade, independent of liquidation capacity; risk is a run on the senior tranche before kFIAT is exhausted. B2 redistribution to survivors (Liquity v1 fallback): your simulations show it recreates the freeze. B3 B1 with a per-epoch ENRK write-down cap and the remainder redistributed under a per-vault cap (two guessed constants). *Proposal:* B1, with ENRK publicly described as "senior, not guaranteed". *To simulate:* swap the waterfall in `stress_test_crash.py` and add ENRK-discount-driven redemption demand (not modelled today).

**C. Freeze and unfreeze without governance.** C1 an auction floor that deepens with time stuck (risk: value transfer to bidders — the Black Thursday lesson). C2 sub-par redemption at min(face, collateral value) for vaults under 100 % ICR (needs no liquidator capital; removes the ~26 % of demand blocked at −95 %). C3 a coded global settlement (terminal, not an unfreeze). *Proposal:* C1 + C2; C3 only as a documented last resort.

**D. Stability Pool.** D1 remove. D2 Liquity-style fix (needs global state). D3 remove it and make auction bids payable in ENRK. *Proposal:* D1 + D3; exclude kFIAT as a bid currency; revisit D2 only if the layer is Igra. (The repository calls the pool's ammunition being the defended asset a flaw; in Liquity v1 that is acceptable. The real defect is that `attempt_buyback` computes a cost and never debits it, `stability_pool.rs:118,127,136`.)

**E. Fee destination after the treasury cap.** E1 burn KAS. E2 pro-rata reserve counted as collateral. E3 route KAS fees to kFIAT holders pro rata (solves the missing junior yield; fees of 1–3 % of debt per year on a 30 % junior give only ~3–10 % of junior face; the yield is paid in KAS, which is wrong-way). *Proposal:* E3 if accrual is expressible on the chosen layer, else E1.

**F. Calibrating the two unmeasured parameters.** (1) Start recording KAS spot order-book snapshots now; the history is not cheaply recoverable later. (2) Fit slippage against √(Q/ADV) for sales of 0.1–5 % of average daily volume to obtain the coefficient. (3) Measure depth shrinkage on stress days as a multiplier. (4) Express capacity as participation × stressed ADV, not a share of debt. (5) Model the tail with a stationary bootstrap and a generalised Pareto fit; KAS has ~3.5 years of history and the realised drawdown from ATH (−83 to −87 % [S]) should not be treated as a tail event. (6) Run a parameter grid (impact 0.04–0.4, capacity 0.5–10 %, demand slope 10–30 %) and choose by minimax regret. (7) Add a hard-coded global debt ceiling or block-height ramp so scale cannot outrun measured liquidity.

## 8. Feasibility verdict

*Layer-specific, honest, and conditional.*

**Kaspa L1 today.** A CDP is expressible with current primitives; Toccata is live; Silverscript v1.0.0 is released. It needs redesign of P2 (binding), P3 (round freshness) and P5 (arithmetic); P1 is a small, compiled fix; P4 is unresolved. Nothing has been run end to end. Verdict: **feasible as a testnet prototype; not shippable as immutable money today** (unaudited toolchain, no mint/burn/binding in KCC-20, no launch-proof tooling, no live comparable application, unknown miner behaviour near full blocks).

**Igra EVM.** Execution is straightforward (aggregate state, recovery mode, pro-rata reserve). Collateral would be bridge-custodied and exit-throttled per the repository's own assessment (not re-verified here), so size would have to stay capped at roughly 6–18 M KAS. Bounded and disclosed, not trustless.

**Economics as an immutable system.** Coherent: it freezes rather than explodes. But quantitative comfort rests on an assumed demand curve, two unmeasured parameters, par kFIAT with no holder incentive and an unimplemented waterfall; immutability removes the usual fix for each. It is viable only if all hold: (1) a hard debt ceiling or ramp sized from measured KAS depth; (2) kFIAT has a defined value and a reason to be held; (3) a capacity-independent loss rule and a deterministic unfreeze path; (4) results robust across the parameter grid; (5) the oracle binding and arithmetic issues are closed — probably a larger risk than the economics. Otherwise it is not viable at scale; even with them, viable at small capped scale with the public claim "senior but not guaranteed".

### 13.1 Consensus and policy (rusty-kaspa v2.1.0 / master, kips @ d832dbd)

- **Toccata:** mainnet activation at DAA score 474,165,565, about 2026-06-30 16:15 UTC; testnet-10 at 467,579,632 (about 2026-05-18). Enabled KIP-16 (`OpZkPrecompile`), KIP-17 (introspection, byte-string and arithmetic ops), KIP-20 (covenant ids), KIP-21 (sequence commitment), tx version 1 with per-input compute budget, gas and lanes. KIPs 9, 10, 13, 16, 17, 20, 21 are "Active". [V] Live-node confirmation *[rev. Nov 10]*: public REST APIs queried read-only on 2026-11-10 report a virtual DAA score of 589,143,174 on mainnet (api.kaspa.org, above 474,165,565) and 619,986,989 on testnet-10 (api-tn10.kaspa.org, above 467,579,632), so both are past activation; consistent with ~133 days at 10 blocks/s since 2026-06-30. Mainnet fee estimate: 100 sompi/gram in every bucket (no fee market). [V-lead, fetched]
- **Mass:** compute = size + 10 × Σ(2 + output script length) + script mass (v1: 100 grams × Σ compute budget); transient = 4 × size; storage per generalised KIP-9 (C = 10¹², plurality p = ceil((63 + script length + 32 if covenant)/100), relaxed formula for 1 output, ≤ 2 inputs of total plurality 1, or 2-in/2-out; otherwise arithmetic mean). Block limits summed per dimension: compute 500,000, transient 1,000,000, storage 500,000; ≤ 50 lanes per block. [V] `consensus/core/src/mass/mod.rs`.
- **No 100,000 standard-mass cap** in v2.1.0 (see P4). Mempool rejects only transactions that can never fit a block; standardness still checks script class, P2SH sigops ≤ 15 and the relay-fee floor. [V]
- **Limits:** 1000 inputs, 1000 outputs, signature script ≤ 250,000 B, script public key ≤ 10,000 B, stack 244, tx version ≤ 1. [V]
- **Fees:** minimum relay fee 100 sompi per gram: `100 × max(compute, 2 × size)`; storage mass is not in the floor; zero fee is consensus-valid. [V]
- **Time:** scripts read `lock_time`, input sequence, the creation DAA score of any spent UTXO (`OpTxInputDaaScore`), CLTV/CSV. They **cannot read the current DAA score or time**. CLTV gives an earliest-spend bound only: **there is no deadline primitive**, so a price UTXO cannot expire by itself. [V]
- **Covenant opcodes:** `OpInputCovenantId` 0xcf (any input index, siblings included), `OpOutputCovenantId` 0xd5, `OpOutputAuthorizingInput` 0xd6, `OpAuthOutputCount/Idx` 0xcb/0xcc (continuation outputs only), `OpCovInputCount/Idx` 0xd0/0xd1, `OpCovOutputCount/Idx` 0xd2/0xd3 (genesis outputs excluded). Consensus checks only that the authorizing input is in range and that the genesis hash is correct; it does **not** force a continuation output to keep the same script or amount, which the covenant script must enforce. `OpCheckSigFromStack` 0xd7 / ECDSA 0xd8 exist. No opcode reads a UTXO without spending it. [V]
- **Network:** 10 blocks per second, GHOSTDAG k = 124, finality depth 432,000 blocks (12 h), merge depth 36,000 (1 h), coinbase maturity 1,000 blocks. [V]
- **Block filling:** candidates are the frontier (no unconfirmed ancestors), weight (fee / max(compute, 0.5 × transient, storage))³; if the frontier ≤ 500 k take all; if > 4×, weighted sampling plus a greedy fill; otherwise weighted draws without replacement. Probabilistic; no inclusion guarantee. [V]
- **Not specified anywhere found:** inclusion guarantees for large transactions; prices for opcodes beyond sigop, pushed byte, hash and ZK; behaviour when a covenant-id stack argument is not 32 bytes; recommended confirmation depth for covenant state; a deadline primitive. [V]

### 13.2 Tooling (Silverscript, Argent)

Oracle-template authentication therefore rests on a statement whose result is unused. It survives only because the alias-lowering pass is disabled (`compile.rs:77-78`, TODO), while the UB specification allows a future compiler to drop it. Mitigation: pin the compiler and audit the bytecode. [V-lead, source read]
- **Known issues:** advisory GHSA-c6jq-qp3r-835q (miscompilation, fixed pre-1.0); open #252 (`readInputStateWithTemplate` plus `validateOutputState` in one entry crashes — the pattern the ENRK vaults use, effect on our output unverified), #249/#228/#227 (struct-array lowering), #243 (no compute-budget estimate). - **Argent security model:** `co_spent()` is a presence check only; Property 7: entries of one actor are indistinguishable to delegates; open ICC fixes only the observed state shape (a hidden mutability channel for an immutable CDP); launch proofs (PR #66, HEAD only) prove which contracts and initial states launched a covenant id, not that a template is correct or funded; KCC-20 bootstrap via `spawns` remains a follow-up. [V]

- **Audit scope required** for an immutable deployment: four layers — ENRK source, Argent-generated `.sil` and route plans, the Silverscript semantics for the subset used, and the deployed bytecode on a pinned engine (rusty-kaspa 2.1.0); tooling to build: per-UB-category negative tests on the real engine, transaction-shape fuzzing, SMT range proofs on the exact opcode sequence, `.sil`-to-opcode diff, reproducible build, worst-case budget and mass tests. [V analysis; no reference methodology exists for Kaspa — U]
- **`UNDEFINED-BEHAVIOUR-EXPOSURE.md` additions:** the unused-local point above; the `.length`-folding and struct-leaf-omission rules; int state fields use the full 8-byte payload; #258 and the stale `pragma ^0.1.0`; HEAD differs from `e76ee07` (#67, #68, #73); the outcome of `probe_const_covid` is not recorded; its "four of eight categories" text lists five and the spec has five headings. ### 13.3b Testnet-10 results (November 10, 2026)

Network use authorised by the owner; a throwaway testnet-10 key was generated for this and funded by the owner from the public faucet (10,000 TKAS). Key material stays in the session scratchpad and is not reproduced here. All facts below are [V-lead, executed]:

Covenant (version 1) transactions need wRPC. The official wRPC client (rusty-kaspa `a41a333`, via `Resolver::default()`) connected to a public testnet-10 node, version 2.1.0, synced. Public Python clients are refused by the REST gateway (403) while `curl` is not; no workaround was used.

  So the oracle–vault construction, with the declared-outputs fix, **works on a real node** for the lifecycle genesis → liquidation, and the N = 256 round (about 39 % of the 500,000 compute limit, about 40 % of transient) was included without delay on a lightly loaded testnet. Caveats: testnet-10 traffic is not mainnet contention; one public node and the sample sizes are small; inclusion of a transaction near 99 % of a block (N ≈ 640) was **not** tested.
- **New constraint found on chain: genesis storage mass.** A genesis of N = 128 price UTXOs of 10 KAS was **refused by the node**: "transaction storage mass of 512000 is larger than max allowed size of 500000". Covenant outputs have plurality 2 (script 35 B + 32 B covenant field → `ceil(130/100) = 2`, so the KIP-9 term is 4 × C / value), i.e. storage ≈ 4 × N × 10¹² / value. A population created in one genesis transaction therefore needs value ≥ 8 × 10⁶ × N sompi per UTXO (N = 256 → ≥ 20.5 KAS each; N = 640 → ≥ 51 KAS each, 32,800 KAS in total). Growing a population by repeated `sweep` splits (variant A leaves values unrestricted) avoids a single large genesis but needs several batched transactions. Rounds that keep values unchanged (`use`, N → N) have no storage mass, as measured. This also refines I19: the "711,040 at N = 640" storage figure concerns value-changing rounds that no entry can produce, while the real storage cost sits in population creation and in `sweep`.
