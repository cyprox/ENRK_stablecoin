Evidence supports t3/v1: with I=1, ENRK p95 13.8, net -15.5; the earlier "safe" conclusions are NOT supported. The cushion is also contradicted by data: a4 weekly corr(KAS, index) -0.13..+0.17, index up while KAS down in 20-29% of 30-day windows, hash rate rose x615; v6 shows the fee leg is pinned at its clip and the adoption leg is undefined. The elasticities r^0.4/r^0.6/r^0.3 come from a Bitcoin analogy in the original simulator docstring, not from Kaspa data. t2 and t1 were never re-based: on flat85 E_w(h=10, w=10%) = 0.9*10.0 + 0.1*(-15.5) = 7.5 < 10, so t2's r_clear = 3.3% and its surviving-demand fractions do not hold on the neutral index.
2. "Surplus to reserve" t1 vs v1. Same direction (S2 pays and protects nothing; S3 protects kFIAT, hardly ENRK), different level: t1 says S3 + r=3 reaches ENRK p95 1.9 (cushioned); v1/my rerun 12.5 on flat85 and 18.4 on x2. v1 is right for the neutral index. 6. Per-vault vs per-transaction capacities (the trap). Liquidation of 180 vaults/tx (AUDIT 13.3b, v3 "~197") was executed with `emits none` outputs. v4 shows the fixed design needs constrained outputs (P11/P12/P19), and then storage binds: 60,977-111,968 storage per liquidation, redemption 3 vaults of 12 KAS per tx (t4/v4), harvest 5. Also v1 P13 uses "ceiling = debt value % of ADV" while r4/v3 quote "100M KAS = 44% of ADV" (collateral/ADV): 22% vs 44% differ by the 200% ICR, not a contradiction, but easy to misquote.

Level of every headline is uncalibrated; ordering of architectures is stable (agree with v1).
8. v4 internal arithmetic error (P20): "10% per GAP = 600 DAA (about a minute) still allows about 1.77x per hour". 1.77 = 1.1^6 (six steps per hour = a 10-minute gap). With 600 DAA at 8.3-11.9 DAA/s (50-72 s) the bound allows 50-60 steps per hour = 1.1^50..1.1^60 = 117x-300x per hour. v3 (0.5% per 600 DAA: 71 steps = 71 min for -30%) is consistent with 1 minute gaps. The conclusion (size GAP and MOVE together) stands; severity understated by two orders of magnitude. Also v3: the price-changing N-to-N refresh is not executed anywhere and the counted-range test FAILED to run; v4's sweep is a 1-to-2 split, so the stale-sibling spread (a consumer picks the stalest sibling) is not bounded by the per-sweep limit.

10. m1/t2/m2 dependence chain: m1's y* (borrower break-even) uses an ASSUMED regime mix and no borrower top-ups; t2 builds demand on assumed y distributions (its own words: "assumptions decide the answer"); m2/t1 junior hurdle uses w=10% crash/yr (assumed). Nothing downstream is data; do not read r_clear or Laffer results as measurements. t2 also notes the argmax of total revenue flips on the assumed 5% liquidation income, itself regime dependent (x3-5, m1).

- P13 ceilings 3-7% of ADV scale linearly with an unmeasured liquidator capital (1-2% ADV/day) and use a convex impact fitted to ONE calm snapshot; 300 paths. Direction (ceiling vs ADV is what matters) OK; the numbers are not usable.

## 4. BLIND SPOTS (ranked)
1. Borrower behaviour under stress (top-ups, exits, repaying ENRK to avoid the 150% line, running before the oracle moves, self-liquidation to capture surplus, reaction to S2/S3 withholding). All sims have passive borrowers; the ICR floor mass at 153% and the r=3% cliff are artefacts of that. A reactive population can move every headline in either direction.
2. Correlated demand: KAS price, ENRK redemption demand, ENRK secondary demand and liquidator capital fall together (capital proportional to price in v1 'adv' mode is the only trace). Redemption demand in the crash is an exogenous schedule that removes ~45% of the modelled hole; no study varies it.
3. Oracle dependence of the unfreeze (C1/C2/P14) and of everything else: the KAS price oracle is a signer-controlled sweep with a stale-sibling spread (v3/v4), no N-to-N refresh executed, per-time move bound mis-stated by 100x, a compromised key can rotate to itself, and the same price decides liquidation, sub-par redemption (C2) and the deepening floor. None of the economic cells models a wrong or lagging price; price-lag stress (crash faster than the allowed move per gap) is untested.
4. Ownership of the single trust surface: publisher(s) of price and index (P8, P15) are the same trust point (v3 says so). Legal/regulatory and trust aspects (a perpetual key in an "immutable" system, fee-paying reserve to individuals, issuer status of a pro-rata reserve) are not studied by any report; FROZEN s7 conflict for P6 b/c/d is noted but not resolved.
5. Data gaps: KAS history is 3.6 years (one bear cycle) with a bear-heavy sample; no Kaspa fee history; no clean adoption series; order book is ONE calm snapshot (r4); wash-trading share unknown; ENRK has no market, so all ENRK demand/elasticity parameters are assumed.
6. Operational/throughput liveness on the constrained design: 180-vault liquidation per tx was executed unconstrained; constrained outputs make storage the bound (1 to ~8 vaults per tx); keeper economics (who pays harvest/refresh: 19-128 USD/day oracle cost) are unfunded; testnet contention was light, mainnet MEV/front-running of the single hot UTXO and of the stale-sibling choice are untested.
7. Tail frequency: latent holes in 4-6% of crashes at stress 0.18 and a cliff between stress 0.25 and 0.28 where the freeze is large even on the cushioned base; stress sensitivity 0.18 is unmeasured. Multi-event sequences (second crash before recovery, repeated shocks) are not simulated; "w=10%/yr crash" is invented.
8. Alternative architectures not benchmarked on the same simulator: debt ceilings sized from depth with auto-ramp, collateral diversification, Liquity-v2-style user-set rates, a USD-fixed index (L3 in v6 is tested only as an ICR variant) combined with fee. The study compares levers to one baseline, not alternative designs end to end.
