## 0. Repo facts used (read in full: FROZEN_PARAMETERS, PHASE_4, REDEMPTION, STRESS-TEST, RECOVERY-MODE, STABLECOIN-FAILURE, KFIAT-DEFINITION-GAP)
- Freeze = no auction clears when demanded discount (4% + 18% x drawdown, an assumed function) exceeds floor discount; floor 75% removes it in the model. Floors 75/70/60 are "identical" only because modelled demand saturates at ~21%; that is a property of the assumed demand curve, not a finding about KAS.
- Redemption (ENRK only, lowest ICR first, min ICR 100%) halves losses but 26% of demand is blocked at -95% (structural: below 100% ICR par cannot be honoured).
- Fees are taken in KAS; treasury 20% to a 2.5M cap then 0%; destination after cap open; reserve rejected in simple form (first-mover/bank run) but pro-rata form needs global state (UTXO problem).
- kFIAT: no producer of kfiat_price, stress test assumes par, seniority waterfall not implemented in submit_bid.
- Two things I could not find in the docs and which matter: (a) a global debt ceiling; (b) any yield/incentive for holding kFIAT ("the protocol pays nothing"). If kFIAT minting is optional (30% is a ceiling) and unpaid, borrowers rationally mint ENRK only, so the junior tranche may be near-empty in practice. UNVERIFIED reading of the repo: check mint_kfiat policy and whether any rule forces a kFIAT share. This is the single most important economic question I found, and it is not on the owner's list.

Cross-cutting lessons (my synthesis):
1. Every surviving design backs the last loss with either a governed treasury (Maker, Aave, Inverse), a valuable token (MKR, FLX), or immutable pro-rata socialisation (Liquity v1 redistribution). ENRK has no valuable governance token and no treasury after the cap, so it is in Liquity v1's family, not Maker's. Recapitalising by minting a token (Maker/Terra pattern) is unavailable and dangerous.
2. Liquity v1's survival depended on three parts working as a set: SP (instant, no keeper), redistribution (last resort), redemption (peg floor), on a deep-liquidity collateral (ETH) with a 110% MCR. ENRK has copied redemption only, with a thin collateral and 150% MCR, which changes the failure geometry.
3. Liquity v2 shows the immutable-design move: turn an unmeasurable governed parameter into a market-discovered one (rate set by each borrower). That principle can be applied to ENRK's riskiest guesses.

## 2. Decision A - kFIAT denomination
Options:
A1 Fixed face in peg units, ICR counts debt at face (KFIAT-GAP Branch 1). Pros: deletes kfiat_price (8 signatures), no new oracle, no reflexive ICR inversion, published stress numbers stay valid, waterfall has meaning. Cons: holders face total-loss tail with no pricing signal on-chain; kFIAT price is purely a secondary-market object; needs yield or discount to be held at all.
A2 Fixed face plus on-chain monotone write-down index (kfiat_index in (0,1], only decreases when the waterfall realises a shortfall). Claim = face x index. ICR still counts vault debt at face (or face x index if the owner wants borrowers relieved; I recommend face, to avoid borrowers benefiting from the loss). No market oracle: the index is computed from protocol accounting only. Pros: kFIAT has a defined, deterministic value; answers "what is a kFIAT worth after a loss" without a second oracle; the existing kfiat_uncovered counter becomes the index. Cons: needs global state (a singleton or epoch accumulator; on UTXO this is a contention point, UNVERIFIED how Kaspa covenants would carry it); index only ratchets down unless a recovery rule is added (decide: no recovery, simplest).
A3 Residual claim marked to market (Branch 2). Pros: honest price. Cons (from KFIAT-GAP, I agree): second oracle, ICR reflexivity (falling kFIAT raises others' ICR), cap formula mixes units, invalidates stress results, double-counting objection holds. Reject.
Failure modes: A1 and A2 hold for kFIAT tokens only if someone buys them: adverse selection (borrowers mint ENRK only) -> empty junior. A3: spiral through kfiat_price in ICR.
Simulate: stress_test with kFIAT secondary price as an output (discount = expected loss under the sim), borrower mint-choice model (what discount makes kFIAT minting rational for 30% share?), holder demand needed for a 10/20/30% junior share.
Proposal for owner decision: A2 (A1 as the minimal fallback). Decide first whether kFIAT minting is forced (e.g. a vault must mint a fixed kFIAT share) or optional; if optional and unpaid, design Decision E so kFIAT earns the fees.

## 3. Decision B - loss-absorption rule
Options:
B1 Waterfall by write-down: shortfall -> kFIAT index down to 0, then ENRK global write-down index (senior haircut). Matches PHASE_4 5.5. Pros: no cascade, deterministic, no extra liquidations; consistent with owner sim "uncapped socialisation to borrowers brings the freeze back". Cons: ENRK stops being par-redeemable once kFIAT is exhausted: run incentive just before exhaustion (redeem early at par). Mitigated because redemption only works at ICR >= 100% and lowest-ICR-first, but expect a rush.
B2 Socialise to surviving borrowers (Liquity v1 redistribution: VERIFIED as fallback when SP empty). Pros: ENRK stays whole; Liquity ran this way. Cons: raises every survivor's debt/ICR pressure, pushing more vaults under MCR in a thin market: the owner's sims say this recreates the freeze. Liquity tolerated it with a 110% MCR, deep ETH liquidity and SP absorbing most liquidations.
B3 Hybrid: kFIAT first, then ENRK write-down capped at X% of ENRK supply per epoch; anything beyond X is redistributed pro rata with a per-vault cap (e.g. no vault absorbs more than Y% of its debt). Pros: bounds both cascade and run; "capped changes little" in the owner's sims means the cap is cheap insurance. Cons: two constants to choose with no measurements; more state; more code in immutable contract.
Failure modes: B1 run on senior; B2 cascade/freeze; B3 parameter risk, complexity.
Simulate: replace the waterfall in stress_test_crash.py with each rule; metrics = P(ENRK impairment > 1/5/10%), vaults liquidated per day, freeze duration, redemption run intensity (add ENRK discount-driven redemption demand, which the doc admits is not modelled), across the Section 7 parameter grid.
Proposal for owner decision: B1, because it needs the least invented parameters and is the only rule whose result does not depend on liquidation capacity; state in public docs that ENRK is senior, not guaranteed. Revisit B3 only if sims show a run on the senior tranche is the dominant loss.

## 4. Decision C - freeze/unfreeze with no governance
Options:
C1 Time-escalating auction floor: the floor deepens automatically with time a vault has been stuck (e.g. 75% -> lower steps after N days without a fill). Pros: self-heals without a keeper of last resort; cheap; no new state beyond a timestamp per auction. Cons: the step schedule is a guess; deeper floor transfers value from ENRK/kFIAT to whoever bids (MEV/oracle-lag gift); Black Thursday zero-bid auctions (SECONDARY) show what an open-ended discount plus thin bidders does.
C2 Sub-par ("pro rata") redemption mode: when a vault is below 100% ICR, allow redemption against it at collateral value (min(face, collateral)) instead of blocking it. Needs no liquidator capital and removes the 26% blocked demand. Pros: ENRK holders exit at fair value; the shortfall is realised as the B1 write-down, so no latent debt; capital-free. Cons: involuntary closing of vaults at a loss to ENRK holders; gives a discount-exit run path; Terra-style capped/limited redemption lesson cuts the other way (repo doc cites uncapped convertibility as requirement).
C3 Coded global settlement (Maker-style emergency shutdown, no vote): if system ratio < 100% for N days, vault creation stops and all debt claims redeem pro rata against collateral per waterfall. Pros: guarantees an exit; terminal, simple. Cons: ends the product (not an unfreeze); trigger parameters again guessed; needs global state.
Failure modes: C1 theft by bidders in a crash; C2 run; C3 premature shutdown by manipulated oracle (Q1c unsolved).
Simulate: freeze duration and loss vs time-since-crash for each, under capacity 0.5-10% of ADV and demand slopes 10-40%.
Proposal for owner decision: C1 + C2 together (floor schedule short and shallow, then sub-par redemption), with C3 only as a documented last-resort if the owner accepts a terminal outcome.

## 5. Decision D - Stability Pool: fix or remove
Note: the repo calls SP ammunition "the asset it defends" a flaw. In Liquity v1 the SP also holds the stablecoin and that is acceptable because burning stablecoin against debt is peg-supportive and depositors are paid in discounted collateral (VERIFIED mechanics above). The real ENRK defect is the implementation bug (burns own balance, no bid).
D1 Remove (repo's preference). Pros: smaller immutable surface, no pro-rata global state (Liquity SP needs product/sum accounting with epochs/scales; hard on UTXO, UNVERIFIED for Kaspa), no bug risk. Cons: liquidation depends 100% on keepers + redemption; no instant liquidity; Recovery Mode stays useless (repo is right: RM pairs with an SP).
D2 Fix as Liquity-style SP. Pros: instant liquidation, strongest known immutable design (running since 2021, SECONDARY). Cons: needs aggregate state; benefit tied to ENRK-holders actually depositing; unproven on thin KAS.
D3 Remove SP but keep a "redemption-first liquidation" path: liquidation of an unhealthy vault can be filled by burning ENRK directly (any holder, at the auction price). It gives SP-like funding without pooled accounting. Pros: no global state; bidder pays in ENRK, which also reduces supply. Cons: that is just auction bids in ENRK, probably already the case (submit_bid takes ENRK/kFIAT as tokens) once token type is specified.
Proposal for owner decision: D1 (remove) and make auction bids payable in ENRK only (kFIAT excluded, see KFIAT-GAP 6.1), unless the layer decision lands on Igra, where D2 should be re-evaluated.

## 6. Decision E - fee destination after the treasury cap
E1 Burn KAS (repo's L1 answer). Pros: zero attack surface, no race, benefits all KAS holders. Cons: ignores kFIAT's missing yield; no loss buffer.
E2 Pro-rata reserve counted as collateral (repo's Igra answer). Pros: no race for the reserve itself; first-loss buffer. Cons: needs global state; reserve still "consumed pro rata" inside a waterfall that senior holders can exit before (redeem at par), so the first-mover problem moves to redemption; oracle-error subsidy risk the repo already identified.
E3 Route fees (KAS) to kFIAT holders pro rata as the junior's return. Pros: directly solves the "who holds first-loss tokens" problem (compare Aave Safety Module rewards and Ethena reserve funded from revenue, both SECONDARY); exogenous asset; no new token. Cons: needs accrual accounting (index); wrong-way risk (the yield is KAS, which falls in the crisis); sizing: fees 1-3% of debt/year (repo) on a 30% junior = 3-10% of junior face per year, maybe thin versus risk (repo's p95 hole 7.5% of debt); and there is a gaming angle (mint kFIAT just before fee distribution) to simulate.
Proposal for owner decision: E3 if the aggregate accrual can be expressed on the chosen layer; otherwise E1. Do not choose E2 unless pro-rata consumption is joined to Decision B.

## 7. Decision F - calibrating impact_coefficient (0.08) and liquidation capacity (5%/day)
What the numbers mean in the model: square-root law dP/P = c * sqrt(Q/V), c = 0.08 (the script comment says a full-volume day gives ~8%); capacity = 5% of opening debt per day.
Measurement plan from Kaspa market data (all read-only, public data; I did not fetch any):
1. Order-book walk: snapshot L2 books (Binance, Kraken, MEXC, Gate, KuCoin spot) every minute going forward via public websockets; also buy historical L2 or trades (Tardis/Kaiko-type vendors; cost UNVERIFIED). For each snapshot compute cost to sell Q = 0.1/0.5/1/2/5% of 30-day ADV; fit slippage vs sqrt(Q/ADV) to get c directly. Separate calm vs stressed snapshots.
2. Impact from tape: estimate c via the square-root-law regression of daily return on signed order flow / sqrt(ADV) (Kyle's lambda style). Reference: the square-root law literature (Toth et al., Bouchaud et al.) typically finds c of order sigma_daily x O(1): UNVERIFIED from memory; check before citing.
3. Stress scaling: from KAS stress days (e.g. the -ATH drawdown of 2024-25, and market-wide cascades) measure how depth within +/-2% shrinks versus a 30-day median; use the shrink factor as a multiplier on c. Need the days list from the data itself.
4. Capacity is not a share of debt; it is a share of market: capacity_day = participation rate x ADV_stress (participation 5-20% of volume is a typical execution assumption, UNVERIFIED) plus whatever keeper balance sheet exists. Then the ratio debt / ADV is the binding quantity: with aggregator ADV ~$10-13M (SECONDARY, half of it low-quality), 5%/day of debt equals ADV once debt ~ $200M, but real stress ADV may be 1/3 of that and real depth much less. A system with, say, $20M debt would need to sell 1M/day: ~10% of ADV, already at the participation edge.
5. Drawdown distribution: KAS has about 3.5 years of history (since 2022 listing, UNVERIFIED start), too short for a p95 -85% claim. Use (a) block/stationary bootstrap of KAS daily returns, (b) GPD peaks-over-threshold on KAS plus analog PoW small caps (RVN, ETC, ZEC, BCH etc.: reported to have lost 90%+ at some point, UNVERIFIED), (c) the realised KAS drawdown (-83 to -87% from ATH, SECONDARY) as a calibration point that should not be a tail event in your model.
6. Replace point values with ranges and report results as a function of them (global sensitivity / Sobol or at least a 3x3x3 grid: c in {0.04,0.08,0.2,0.4}, capacity in {0.5,1,2.5,5,10}% of ADV or debt, demand slope in {10,18,30}%); pick designs by minimax regret, not by best-case numbers.
7. Because the protocol is immutable, introduce a hard-coded global debt ceiling or time-based ramp (launch cap, step-ups by block height, not by vote) so that debt/ADV is bounded whatever the measurements turn out to be. Maker's debt ceilings are governed (UNVERIFIED), so this is an ENRK-specific substitute. PROPOSAL: this is the cheapest hedge against unmeasured parameters.
Proposal for owner decision: collect the order-book data now (it is cheap and unrecoverable later), and treat 0.08 and 5%/day as untrusted until step 1-2 results exist.

## 8. Other items worth a decision (not asked, but relevant)
- Redemption ordering: Liquity v2 (VERIFIED) redeems lowest borrower-set rate first, so those at risk chose it and pay for it; ENRK's lowest-ICR-first dispossesses the least collateralised involuntarily (repo admits it). Option: market-set redemption priority. Larger redesign; list only.
- Oracle binding (Q1c) and undefined behaviour in Argent sources are security risks on immutable code; they likely dominate economic risk, and the economics assume a correct oracle.
- Settlement dimension bug (KFIAT-GAP 6.2) and unimplemented waterfall mean none of the stress results has been checked against code.

## 9. Verdict (honest)
The design is internally coherent about one thing: it freezes rather than explodes, and a 75% floor plus redemption roughly halves modelled losses. But all quantitative comfort rests on (i) an assumed liquidator-demand curve, (ii) two unmeasured market parameters, (iii) par kFIAT with no holder incentive, and (iv) an unimplemented waterfall. None is verified. Immutability removes the usual remedy for each.
Viable as an immutable system only if ALL of these hold:
1. A hard debt ceiling or ramp, sized from measured KAS depth/volume (Section 7), so scale cannot outrun liquidity.
2. kFIAT has a defined value (A2) and a reason to exist (forced share and/or fee yield, E3); otherwise the junior tranche is empty and ENRK is effectively unprotected.
3. A loss rule that does not depend on liquidation capacity (B1) and a deterministic unfreeze path (C1+C2).
4. Results shown robust across the parameter grid, not at the 0.08/5% point.
5. The oracle binding and arithmetic issues are closed.
Without these, I would call it not viable at scale; with them, viable at a small, capped scale where the honest public claim is "senior but not guaranteed". Not viable: any version that recapitalises by minting tokens (Maker/Terra pattern) or relies on a governed treasury.
Caveats of this report: primary pages were fetched only for Liquity v1 SP and v2 redemptions; everything else is search-summary level or memory; no KAS order-book data was obtained; Angle and Frax were not researched.
